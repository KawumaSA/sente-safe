Mobile money tricks

These tricks go straight for your mobile money account or your identity.

Fake prizes and PIN scams

You have won something you never entered, or someone “sent money by mistake”.

The prize-draw scam

A caller says you won a large prize from a famous company and must pay a small fee first. One of our group received a call like this in 2024, with a prize of UGX 3,000,000 and a fee of UGX 200,000 [7].

A simple test. Say: “Take the fee out of my prize and send me the rest.” A genuine prize can be handled that way. A scammer will refuse, get angry or hang up, which is what happened to our group member.

Dear customer, your number won UGX 5,000,000 in our monthly draw. To claim, send UGX 100,000 tax fee to [number]. Do not tell anyone.

“Sent by mistake”

You get a message that looks like a money transfer, then a call: “I sent that to the wrong number, please send it back.” The message may be fake. Check your balance in your own mobile money menu. Never rely on a message someone else forwarded.

PIN scams

Phishing, SIM swaps and fraudulent SMS asking for your PIN are the common schemes the Bank of Uganda has warned about [4]. Nobody who works for your provider needs your PIN.

Rule: never tell anyone your PIN or the code sent to your phone, even someone who knows your name or sounds like a friend.

SIM cards and your National ID

Why SIM cards matter, and how lending your ID can put you in trouble.

Mobile money scams run on SIM cards. In the 2026 court case the man used SIM cards registered in other people’s names, and was convicted of using another person’s SIM card for an illegal purpose [1].

Your legal risk

UCC reminds people that a SIM registered in your name is your responsibility. If you give your National ID to someone else to register a SIM, you carry the legal risk if it is used for crime [1].

  • Do not let strangers use your ID to register SIM cards, even for a small payment.
  • Keep copies of your ID safe and do not post photos of it online.

SIM swap

In a SIM swap, a fraudster gets a replacement SIM for your number so calls, texts and mobile money codes go to them. The Bank of Uganda lists SIM swaps among the common schemes [4]. If your phone loses network for no clear reason, contact your provider straight away.

Lost or stolen phone or SIM

  1. Tell your provider at once so the SIM and mobile money can be blocked.
  2. Report it to the police.
  3. Ask your provider how to get a replacement SIM registered in your name.

UCC advises reporting lost or stolen phones and SIM cards to your provider and the police immediately [1].
